# Synthetic Behavioral Checks

These are fictional skill tests, not current company evidence or investment
recommendations. Use the skill and reusable analyst brief to answer each case
without additional data collection. State unsupported inputs and return useful
conditional judgments rather than completing missing facts by invention.

## A: A discount and refinancing risk

Shares trade at 100. A prior, unchanged central intrinsic-value estimate is 160.
The same FY2028 operating-profit consensus fell from 12 to 10 to 8 over equally
spaced vintages. Customer funding spreads widened; refinancing is pending.
There is no direct flow dataset and no accessible whisper survey. The next
results arrive in six weeks. Assess the entry decision and what would change it.

## B: A premium and improving future expectations

Shares trade at 130 versus central intrinsic value of 115. An independently
supported forecast exceeds same-period consensus; estimate revisions are
accelerating. A new contract could extend profitable reinvestment duration.
There is evidence of broader sector participation and balance-sheet capacity.
No return distribution, transaction costs, or loss budget is supplied. Assess
whether a buy scenario is admissible and whether a numeric buy range is ready.

## C: A beat with a negative reaction

Reported revenue growth is 20%, down from 30% in the previous comparable period.
Pre-event published consensus expected 10%. No sourced whisper is available.
Shares fall 5% while their sector falls 4%; a large share issuance was announced
on the same day. Explain the implications for operating performance, surprise,
market recognition, and the next discriminating evidence.

## D: Recognized versus unrecognized success

At a shared horizon the business success probability is 0.60. Conditional on
success, recognition probability is 0.50, with prices 150 if recognized and 105
otherwise. Business failure probability is 0.40, with price 70 regardless of
recognition. Current price is 100. Ignore distributions and costs for this test.
Calculate the leaf probabilities and expected price/return. Explain what cannot
be inferred about an entry ceiling after a change in market state.

## E: Hindsight, rolling estimates, and opaque positioning

A June decision is being reviewed with charts first published in October.
An August filing describes June-end long puts by underlying-security value.
Today's NTM EPS is above last month's, but the NTM window and broker cohort both
changed. Shares are up 30% with market capitalization up 30 billion. Identify
what these observations do and do not establish, and propose obtainable tests.
