# Learning principles

Review the quality of a decision using the evidence available at the time,
separately from whether the outcome was profitable. If the outcome is already
known, acknowledge hindsight exposure. Separate missing data, flawed reasoning,
rule noncompliance, implementation costs, and ordinary uncertainty.

Preserve strengths as repeatable behaviors with stated applicability limits.
For weaknesses, propose a bounded alternative and identify counterexamples
and competing explanations. A single win or loss is insufficient evidence of
a durable advantage. Strength does not imply greater leverage or exposure.

Treat improvements as hypotheses until tested on independent unseen or forward
cases, including costs, adverse regimes, no-trade cases, and failed trials.
Keep the previous baseline and explicit failure criteria. Do not silently
replace principles, claim proven profitability, or treat confidence as proof.
