# Equity Valuation

Use this method for company valuation and price-target work. Preserve an
explicit user-selected method or horizon, and do not force equity cash-flow
models onto instruments or businesses they cannot support.

## Story, evidence, and estimates

Use a Damodaran-style story-to-numbers approach: describe the business model,
competitive position, and lifecycle, then connect that story to revenue
growth, operating margins, reinvestment, and risk. Cash-generation-based
intrinsic value is the primary anchor; trading comparables are a cross-check.
Test current-price expectations, using reverse DCF before forward DCF when
the business and evidence support it.

For Korean-company reported financials, inspect original DART filings first,
then obtain forward consensus estimates. Record issuer, report/receipt,
publication date, financial period, consolidated/separate basis, statement or
note, currency, and units. Never use blogs as evidence for reported figures.
If DART cannot be accessed, disclose the gap; an original KRX KIND filing may
support the claim with its actual location identified. For other jurisdictions,
prefer the relevant official filings and issuer disclosures. Reuse adequate
current-workflow evidence instead of recollecting it.

Distinguish reported actuals, issuer guidance, external consensus, and analyst
estimates. Check the source date and coverage of forecasts. Consensus is a
comparison, not a substitute for testing the business story. If a forecast,
period, or metric is missing or unusable, construct an explicit estimate from
the available evidence. Do not silently substitute trailing earnings for
missing forward estimates. Missing consensus is not itself a reason to stop;
missing underlying evidence still limits confidence and decision readiness.

Estimate revenue from appropriate operating drivers, such as volume and
price, customers and revenue per customer, or backlog conversion. Connect
costs, utilization, margins, depreciation, CAPEX, and working capital
consistently. Bridge enterprise value to common equity using relevant debt,
cash, other claims, and diluted shares. Do not invent historical inputs or
present assumed forecasts as verified data.

## Comparable periods

For both the subject and peers, use future estimates over matching actual
dates. Default to NTM, or choose a common calendar year when it improves
comparability and explain why. State the valuation date and forecast
start/end dates. A next-four-quarter approximation must name its quarters;
it is not exactly a rolling twelve months from the valuation day.

Fiscal-year labels alone do not establish comparability. Calendarize using
quarterly estimates where available; otherwise construct the missing periods
with seasonality and operating drivers rather than an unexplained linear
allocation. Estimate missing peer coverage as well as subject coverage.

Compute peer multiples using market values from the same valuation date and
earnings from the selected common future period; apply the selected multiple
to the subject's same-period estimate. Keep accounting adjustments and the
enterprise/equity-value numerator and denominator consistent. Explain peer
selection and growth, profitability, reinvestment, and risk differences;
avoid unsupported premiums, discounts, and double counting growth.

## Scenarios and probabilities

Build upside, base, and downside cases from internally consistent stories and
operating assumptions, not fixed percentage changes to a central price.
Use current market conditions, relevant rates/liquidity/cycle/industry supply
and demand, company-specific evidence, catalysts, and appropriate base rates
to assign probabilities. Cite the evidence and its as-of date. Do not repeat
fixed weights without reasoning or let sentiment alone determine intrinsic value.

Define non-overlapping representative outcome categories over the same
horizon and make probabilities sum to 100%. Include material failure,
financing, dilution, or distress outcomes within the downside category.
Explain the estimation method and uncertainty. Judgment-based probabilities
are assumptions, not observed facts or validated calibration. When precision
is weak, give provisional weights and probability sensitivity, and preserve
the resulting readiness limit. Do not confuse these probabilities with
risk-neutral or market-implied probabilities.

## Targets and expected value

State the target date separately from the forecast earnings period. Unless
the user supplies another horizon, default to twelve months after the
valuation date and disclose that choice.

Present each case's main assumptions, probability, same-date per-share
target, and price return from the sourced current price. Derive scenario
targets with consistent cash/debt and dilution assumptions. Show the current
intrinsic-value estimate separately from a future-date target. If future
intrinsic value serves as a market-price target, explain the assumed
convergence or residual discount. Apply time-value adjustments when needed
without discounting an already-current forward-multiple valuation again.

Expected target = sum of (scenario probability x scenario target).
Expected price return = expected target / current price - 1.
Use decimal probabilities. All targets in the expectation must share the
same date, currency, and per-share basis. If presenting total return, include
scenario-specific expected distributions explicitly and distinguish it from
price return. Keep all three cases visible alongside the expectation.

Report the most consequential sensitivities, contrary evidence, falsifiers,
and events that would change targets or weights. Valuation and a favorable
expected return do not establish portfolio suitability or execution readiness.
